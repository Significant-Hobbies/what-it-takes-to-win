## Method and source boundaries

The first pass follows the five named priority leads, including disconfirming results.
The second is a finite three-lead technical/context slice; it does not expand the intake.
Sources have exact URLs, locations, publication-date precision, access dates,
origin/dependence notes and retrieval status. Claims reference sources; timeline
entries reference claims. An access success is not evidence approval.

The earlier 13 October access record remains in `source-access.json`. It records
13 attempts, 11 retrieved responses and two HTTP 403 responses; those hashes
remain historical retrieval evidence, not a claim that every source was reread.
The ledger records 45 sources and 54 claims across the 14 and 16 October passes. Search extracts and
failed retrievals are explicit; they cannot support recorded claims. Two PDFs
that the browser extractor could not read were retrieved directly: Spinks’s
later account and the original 2004 race scan. The latter's four pages, five
FRA magazine pages across four issues, and the Macomber bibliography's first page
were inspected visually. The WMC table was
read through text extraction; its visual cross-check remains open.

No access controls were bypassed. The Hallmark PDF's certificate mismatch was
not bypassed. No private profiles, paid services, interviews or outreach were used.
Copyrighted articles are linked with short paraphrases, not copied into the repo.
Unnecessary sensitive personal disclosures are not reproduced.

**Core boundary:** earliest qualifying external proof at 26–39 inclusive;
earlier qualifying proof in any domain excludes core inclusion. Located results
are upper bounds until earlier history is audited. Ordinary work, education,
practice, school difficulty and caregiving do not establish either distinction
or its absence. No probability, prevalence, universal percentile or quota score
is calculated.

### 001 — Dominic O’Brien

See DO1–DO6. O’Brien's own history (R33) calls a six-deck, zero-error 1988
attempt his first world record. The Recordholders.org table (R35) lists 35
inter-shuffled decks, 1,820 cards and two errors in 1990. The World Memory
Sports Council's 2016 retrospective (R34) calls him a 35-deck Guinness holder
before its 26 October 1991 championship. This corrects the chronology: 1991
was not his first located externally recognised memory result. The 1988
attempt remains a first-person claim without its original adjudication record;
the 1990 record is separately tabulated but its original Guinness entry and
exact acceptance date were not retrieved. Do not label either as the settled
first qualifying result yet.

R34 names seven ranked finishers in 1991 and describes seven contest events,
but also names eight competitors. It does not explain the difference or
give the full entry pool or original scores. Its PDF text was readable
through the browser extractor; direct retrieval returned HTTP 403, so the
historical table still needs visual review. The retrospective and R01 are
organiser publications, not independent witnesses. Current rankings cannot
supply historical rules. A training start at a reported age does not establish
a birthday. Employment, sport, arts, technical and community history before 26
remain open; the school account provides partial coverage only. Same-name
engineering results are not attributed to the memory competitor without
identity evidence.

### 004 — Nicola “Nicky” Spinks

See NS1–NS14. Five visually checked FRA magazine pages precede the 2004
Heart of Granite scan. They list N. Spinks third woman in the 15 August 2002
Denis Stitt race (50th/69), fourth woman in the 9 September Trunce final
(43rd/93), and first woman in Trunce 1 on 31 March 2003 (83rd/207, eight
listed women). A June 2003 Hunshelf organiser narrative expands the name to
Nicky Spinks; its table lists her first of five women (35th/62). The July
Broomhead result lists her first of three women (22nd/56). The early
initial-only entries are a probable identity match, not direct identity proof.
The March win is the earliest *located category win*, not a settled earliest
qualifying result; the category's competitive standard and any earlier wins
still require review. The 2004 scan lists her eighth in 09:01:12 among 11
finishers plus eight retired entries, and its original report is attributed
to organiser Colin Butler though hosted by Spinks.

These 2002 entries precede Spinks's later recollection of discovering the
Trunce while preparing for a run by October 2002. A separate intake account
says 2001. The retrospective sequence, initial-only attribution and earliest
race date remain unresolved; neither later memory nor a search omission can
erase the contemporary results. Her own 2017 account of running the Joss
Naylor Challenge on her fiftieth birthday, 22 April, implies 22 April 1967.
A sponsor reported that birthday two days after the event, but appears to
depend on Spinks's statement. The resulting age 35 at the March 2003 win is
provisional, not a certified or independently authenticated birth finding.

Penistone Footpath Runners & AC independently lists Spinks among its 2005
completers and gives 23:33. The club's timing source is unstated, and its page is
not an individual Bob Graham Club record. The 23:32 in her later account remains
unexplained; the original timing sheet and direct ratification remain targets.

The first-female prize story and local 2003 category wins need their actual
comparison pools and recognition standards. A category label is not proof of
the collection's excellence threshold. The 2001 versus 2002 first-fell
chronology and 23:32 versus 23:33 round times stay unresolved.
The 2022 England Athletics interview adds Spinks's recollection of running in
childhood and again at 19, plus horse riding and cycling. It improves the list
of pre-26 activity to check but supplies no independent early result or birth
record. The Bob Graham Club's indexed member-list lead was not readable directly
because of a certificate mismatch and retrieval timeout, so it supports no
ledger claim. No independent birth record or individual club ratification was obtained.

## Reuse, corrections and review

The ledger deliberately lives outside runtime publication inputs. It stores:
identity and alias handling; birth precision; claim/source links; separate
capability and proof events; working conditions and unknown hours; attempts;
subsequent outcomes; six explicit earlier-history domains; searches actually
performed; contradictions; hold reasons; next checks; review state and corrections.
Empty attempt/outcome arrays mean no item documented in this pass, not no attempts
or subsequent success. Null earlier-proof/age-25 fields mean unknown, never false. True earlier proof
excludes core inclusion; it does not approve a contrast profile. `intakeId: null`
marks an unregistered research lead, whose `draft-` ID cannot impersonate an intake ID.

Each claim must cite retrieved material. Source origins are claim-dependent:
R12's publisher record and its quoted life testimony have different evidentiary
roles. R13 independently corroborates a publication, not the subject's complete
history. Do not mechanically count domain names or source IDs as corroboration.
Keep event dates distinct from article/upload dates and preserve precision.
Corrections should reference the claim and explain what changed; Git retains
prior wording. The draft schema does not permit publication approval fields.

Independent second review is pending and will challenge dates, attribution,
selectivity, source independence and coverage limits. Automated validation and
a second agent's review of this draft alone cannot clear absent evidence.
After that challenge, integrate only substantiated claims into the existing
review model, preserving the workbook, stable IDs and provenance. Any membership
change must pass its existing publication blockers and rights/consent review.
